🔍 In plain words

Study and High_low are labels: you can only count how many people are in each box. Earnings and Earnings_lastyear are amounts of money: you can add them up and take an average.

👥 One more thing to notice

Earnings and Earnings_lastyear were measured on the same 100 students. That makes them a pair of measurements. Remember this for Question 4.

🪝 Memory hook

A label is a name badge; a number is a price tag. You can count name badges, but you can only average price tags.

🗣️ Explain it to a friend

"Before I open SPSS, I underline three things: what I have to calculate, which variable, and which group. If the question asks for a p-value, a test statistic or whether something is different, it's a test. If it just asks 'what is the mean or proportion', I only describe."

⌨️ All eight in SPSS syntax

* Q1 and Q2 (descriptives per group).
MEANS TABLES=Earnings BY Study /CELLS=MEAN COUNT STDDEV.
MEANS TABLES=Earnings_lastyear BY High_low /CELLS=MEAN COUNT STDDEV.
* Q3 independent-samples t-test.
T-TEST GROUPS=High_low(0 1) /VARIABLES=Earnings.
* Q4 paired-samples t-test.
T-TEST PAIRS=Earnings WITH Earnings_lastyear (PAIRED).
* Q5 proportion.
FREQUENCIES VARIABLES=High_low.
* Q6 and Q7 crosstab with row % and expected counts.
CROSSTABS /TABLES=Study BY High_low /CELLS=COUNT EXPECTED ROW.
* Q8 chi-square goodness-of-fit against 40% Low / 60% High.
NPAR TESTS /CHISQUARE=High_low /EXPECTED=40 60.
